# Lane 4: escapements and miscellaneous plates (217, 269, 272, 277, 292, 297, 304, 308, 351)

This pass compares each model with Brown's plate (default capture beside
`public/engravings/mm_NNN.png`) and screens rendered-body intersections with
`show-body-intersections` at spacing 0.01 over 129 samples. "Clear" below means
no sampled solid pair at that spacing, which is triage evidence for the sampled
phases, not a continuous collision certificate.

## 308 — rebuilt to Brown's plate

The former model was Airy's sixty-pin wheel with a long pendulum rod and bob,
none of which the plate draws. It is replaced by what the plate shows: the
broad pendulum P, P (two bowed uprights joined by a web, broken off top and
bottom), a six-toothed hooked escape wheel under a screwed cock, bell-crank
lever Q on a stud, click C pivoted on the web with its two stop pins, and
pallet I screwed to the web. Outlines are traced at 0.018 model units per raster
pixel; the default view is the front elevation and t = 0 is the drawn instant
(C just meeting Q on the leftward swing).

Motion is solved from finite 2D outlines by a stepped projection (tables over
pendulum travel): each step Q falls toward rest, the wheel tends to advance and
C springs back, and each is held to the nearest pose that keeps 0.002 clearance.
On the leftward swing C lifts Q, the released wheel drops onto the nib of
pallet I and drives it (impulse), and Q relocks the next tooth; on the return C
pivots aside under Q. One tooth (60°) per pendulum cycle, no wheel motion on
the return. Interpolated playback keeps at least 0.00075 clearance (tests).

Inferred, not drawn: the nib standing up from pallet I into the wheel plane,
the round lock pin on Q, C's round tip, the pivot studs, and pendulum
translation (its suspension is outside the plate). The lock has no draw: the
wheel creeps about 3.5° as Q lifts. Q's rest banking and C's spring are not
drawn and are prescribed; impulse energy and impacts are not simulated.

## 292 — arms G and H, handoff relieved

The two pallet arms now follow the plate: G drops steeply behind the wheel to
the rear pallet and H runs out to B in front and back to the front pallet. The
working blocks' lock/impulse offset-curve cusp (cut 0.0016 deep by the studs)
is relieved by the sampled stud discs: the 2,049-pose stud sweep now clears by
0.00021 and the rendered screen is clear (was 0.0006). The contact marker and
index marks, which the plate does not draw, are hidden. The full wheel stays in
view although Brown crops it; the studs remain cylinders where Brown draws
small triangles.

## 297 — plain disc

Both lantern end plates are plain bored discs, as drawn, with no spokes; the
pins are light so they read as the plate's circles. Index and contact markers
are hidden. Arm A remains in front of the disc where Brown dashes it as hidden.
Screen clear.

## 351 — release and pickup

After release the rack is stepped under gravity and held to the nearest height
clear of the finite teeth, so it follows the withdrawing final tooth (which
keeps lifting it; carried apex 3.85 instead of 3.57) before falling from that
later release. The entering tooth's trailing flank reached the resting rack
0.0033 deep, so the stop plane (workpiece and anvil) is 0.008 lower and the
entering tooth picks the rack up. The lower rod is 0.36 longer so the head and
collar stay below the lower guide at the carried apex. The pinion teeth no
longer fill the shaft passage. Screen: worst was 0.139 (tooth/rack), now clear;
cycle tooth clearance ≥ 0.00049. The rod and guides remain slimmer than drawn.

## 217 — cam alone, e at twelve o'clock

Plate 217 shows only the grooved cam, so its presentation removes the notch
wheel F, rocker, stud A, catch G and markers (218 is unchanged). The cam now
starts with e at twelve o'clock. The groove is still the exact locus of stud A
on the rocker about H, not Brown's symmetric heart: a lever carrying A about H
cannot sweep the captioned 240° (one third back, two thirds forward) through a
single-valued heart, so matching both caption and heart is impossible with the
drawn linkage.

## 269 — generated rack teeth

The straight-flank teeth were not conjugate (about 0.04 interference in plain
rolling, 0.069 at handoffs). The pinion keeps Brown's square, parallel-sided
teeth; every rack tooth is generated by relieving its straight outline with the
pinion swept through the whole prescribed stroke (0.002 trigger, 0.006 grown
cutters). The relief is baked in the factory (checked against its generator by
a test). Minimum sampled tooth clearance 0.0038; screen clear. Because both
racks' groups abut and the deep teeth reach about two pitches either side, all
sixteen rack teeth near handoffs are visibly cut back, unlike the plate's square
teeth. The frame's closed end still sits farther right than drawn.

## 272 — cone rim and wavy face

The cam is a disk whose rim is a cone parallel to the rod and whose front face
is a trough x = H(y): seen from the side its edge is Brown's wavy line. The rod
therefore rises and falls twice per turn (stroke 0.347). The shoe/face contact
is solved numerically on the trough (0.0005 running clearance); rim clearance
stays ≥ 0.17. The default view is the plate's side elevation; shaft bearings,
index marks and the contact marker, which are not drawn, are removed/hidden.
Screen clear. The gravity preload is assumed.

## 304 — broad plate on a disk

The thin V arms, grey standard, bracket and base are gone. A broad plate hangs
from a collet (centre ring, two slotted screws) in front of the pin ends (0.05
clearance), with a V opening between its legs; each leg ends in a pallet bit
and a small screwed plate sits on the right leg. Each bit is a generous block
with every pin pose over the cycle subtracted (0.003 clearance); B pins keep
only the working arc (Brown's thin wedge); pin stems run through the rim. The
default view is a flat 8° elevation. With Goodrich's pin size the 4°/2°
impulse/drop split is impossible, so pins roll over a rounded pallet tip
(about 0.90° outer and 1.23° inner lift) and drop the rest. Screen: worst 0.1193
→ clear. Residuals: pallets 6° apart where Brown draws about 13°; A pins face
the direction of motion; locks are thin slivers; motion is prescribed.

## 277 — rigid bored dog

Dog a is a rigid plate on a pin in a bored eye on the tumbler; ratchet b is a
closed six-tooth ratchet with radial driving faces on the cylinder's rear;
spring c is a stiff bowed leaf turning about its root under the traced hatched
block. Motion is stepped once with 0.005 clearance: exactly 60° per cock, no
back-turning, the hook rides over the next tooth on the fall. The receiver
lock, wards, markers, base and posts are removed (an undrawn detent is
assumed). Screen: worst 0.139 → clear (also at spacing 0.005). Residuals: the
hook works lower than drawn, ratchet teeth are shallow (a thin band from the
side), hammer stroke is 30° instead of 42.3°, the spring rotates rigidly.
