# Movement 391: upper selector contact

Primary reference: [Brown, movement 391](https://507movements.com/mm_391.html), its caption and local engraving `public/engravings/mm_391.png`. The page has no working 2D animation. This pass supersedes the elbow-selector residual in [the preceding rack clearance review](weighted-rack-391-contact-review.md); that pass's shorter stroke and full-sized rack/weight geometry remain unchanged.

The engraving shows a small **inboard upper lug** on A1, distinct from its outboard guide pin. Spring d pulls C's lower arm to the right, so the working face must push A1 **outward** over the upper corner. The preceding assignment instead moved A1 inward while C assisted it, giving the attempted cam the wrong torque. Brown assigns one rack to ascent and the other to descent without naming which: this reconstruction reverses the working-side assignment, making A1 work on ascent and A on descent. The output now advances +0.8 turn per piston cycle, continuously through repeated cycles; the marked wheel still closes after five piston cycles. This direction is an inference from the spring and lug arrangement, not an independently animated oracle.

C is now a closed, bored curved plate with a rounded lower entry. A separate bored lug and roller meet its circular working face. Circle/circle tangency solves the lever angle directly, including its rounded entry: there is no iterative mesh/contact search or live physics. The rising lug loads the spring from about 2.278 s; the lever assists outward selection from 3.36 to about 3.622 s, then its stem meets a real fixed stop and the lug separates. A small fixed bracket carries the pivot, stop and spring anchor. The ideal circular arc and inferred axial stand-offs replace ambiguous engraved edge details; they are not traced dimensions.

Validation (`node --test tests/movement-391.test.mjs tests/weighted-rack-handoff-solids.test.mjs tests/weighted-rack-selector-contact.test.mjs`): **20/20 pass**.

- The previous 257-pose rack/weight/pinion and 65-pose guide/input/bearing sweeps still pass with the reversed sequence. No teeth were shortened or removed.
- The additional 257-pose actual-solid sweep covers cam/roller, cam/outboard guide pin, cam/lug and rack body, the bored pivot, roller axle, spring stud, stop and bracket interfaces.
- Independent nearest-triangle normals on the visible working face give outward rack moment from −2.94 to −4.16 per unit normal force across 52 assist samples. The contact reaction opposes the spring moment, allowing a **compressive** quasistatic reaction. Maximum sampled face/roller gap is 8.22e−6, from polygonal approximation. Clearance alone is not the evidence for assistance.
- Contact loading, assist, release and rest-stop engagement are checked separately. An 8,193-pose sweep checks continuity and the analytic nonpenetration condition. Scene objects and geometry buffers are retained.
- Default/front/loading/assist/release browser captures show no errors; sampled full-cycle maximum normalized screen extent is 0.858. RAM evidence: `/dev/shm/rack29-final-391-*.png` and `/dev/shm/rack29-final-browser-review.json`.
- Local Node timing: approximately 0.0107 ms per update over 10,000 warmed updates; approximately 585 ms construction, including the existing guide geometry. The new selector requires no offline collision-mesh build or baked trajectory.

The **rack trajectories and zero-speed guide-corner timing remain prescribed**. This proves finite selector topology, contact closure, appropriate force direction and clean release on those trajectories; it does not prove free spring/gravity-driven selection of both guide branches. Spring preload/mass, friction, inertia, impacts at entry/stop, and output load are not specified or integrated. In particular, the opposite rack's imposed corner transfer remains an idealization. No MuJoCo result or complete passive-force qualification is claimed. The frame attachments outside the small selector bracket remain schematic. These limits are also exposed in the model's selector and dynamics metadata.
