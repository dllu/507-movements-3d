# Cord and traverse family: 352, 358, 362

Reviewed against the official [352](https://507movements.com/mm_352.html), [358](https://507movements.com/mm_358.html), and [362](https://507movements.com/mm_362.html) captions and engravings. All three official pages mark their Animated tab unavailable (checked 2026-09-16); there is no available official 2D oracle for this pass.

## Corrections

- **352:** retained the existing spatial tangent construction and differential-windlass law. Three closed, bored sheaves now have actual rope channels; the small barrel and its flanges accommodate the complete wound pack. Short axial lead-in ramps replace the whole-pack easing that crowded adjacent rope turns. Shaft bearings have real aligned bores, and the hanger occupies a separate depth from the rope while reaching its axle.
- **358:** replaced the raised decorative spiral with a closed, bored fusee containing a finite channel. Opposed cords occupy separated tracks within this channel, including separate takeoff positions, rather than overlapping at a common finite-thickness point. Carriage wheels now meet their rails; their bored hubs receive axles. Shaft journals connect to the carriage frame. The original ten-revolution radius/travel law remains. The source-facing horizontal fusee uses a carriage-following closeup with rail marks; the full track remains in the scene and is reachable by zooming out. Following changes the presentation frame, not relative mechanism geometry.
- **362:** replaced a raised line on a solid cylinder with a finite closed oblique groove and bored end plates. The rounded follower fits between retaining flanks. An oblique plane section of a circular cylinder produces sinusoidal axial displacement; this replaces the previous invented triangular law and instantaneous velocity reversals. The unseen return half is inferred as the continuation of that plane. Input rotation remains uniform and the upper drum translates without invented rotation. The follower phase and rear supports now present the engraving's upper-left/lower-right arrangement from the visible working side.

All three suppress ground/fog and retain readable authored cycle minima (8.4, 12, and 8 seconds). The 352/358 rope meshes now deform retained, capped GPU buffers instead of replacing tube geometry during playback. A local 120-update CPU measurement averaged approximately 0.80/1.19/0.008 ms for 352/358/362; this is a local timing observation, not a hardware-independent performance guarantee.

## Qualification and limits

`tests/cord-traverse-working-solids.test.mjs` checks both directions of sampled rendered surfaces: 352 rope against barrels/flanges/sheaves/hanger; 358 both cords against the actual fusee and one another; 362 follower/stem against groove/rims, and shafts/drum against journals. Deforming rope vertices and triangle BVHs are refreshed per pose. Further checks require solid groove walls, actual axial flank capture when the pin is displaced, open shaft bores, separated adjacent windlass turns, wheel/hub fit, retained buffers, and the unchanged carriage travel law. Legacy movement tests cover tangent/no-slip identities and the revised smooth traverse derivatives. Root's camera regression additionally checks full-track zoom-out in portrait orientation.

This is selected-interface geometric and analytical qualification, not an all-pairs collision or force simulation. Cord tension, stretching, slip, and exact material-length changes from finite helical lay and the separated takeoffs remain approximations. In particular, the old coincident-takeoff identities describe the ideal pitch law; they do not prove conservation of each newly separated rendered cord's length. The narrow end of the fusee has a thin inferred wall and does not establish manufacturing strength. The 362 follower has running clearance, so flank preload, backlash, friction and loaded reversal dynamics remain prescribed rather than contact-solved. No MuJoCo bake is claimed or required for these bounded analytic corrections.

Browser artifacts and detailed timing reports remain in `/dev/shm`, outside Git. The 358 closeup intentionally crops remote track ends; all moving carriage geometry fits throughout the cycle, and zoom-out retains the complete mechanism.

Validation: the initial combined run passed 31/31 tests. After the final 362 bed-depth correction, all 10 affected 362 checks passed, including the new whole-foot support and actual bearing/bridge/post join checks (32 family checks now exist). Final browser review reported no errors; maximum projected carriage extent for the intentional 358 closeup was 0.777 NDC, and the complete 362 mechanism was 0.804 before the final bed shift. Parent integration rechecks the packaged 362 after that shift. Movement 352's complete-cycle initial view measured 0.819 NDC.
