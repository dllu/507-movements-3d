## Findings

### Range-wide: near-black shafts read as holes (medium)
- **Flaw:** in 74 of the 85 movements (every one except 86–88, 102, 103, 105, 108–111 and 160), shafts, pins, pivot shafts and bolts use `PALETTE.ink` (#252a2d, `src/simulation/primitives.js:9`). Seen end-on, a shaft reads as an empty black hole where Brown draws a solid hatched shaft. This is the "white pegs read as holes" problem in reverse. It is also inconsistent with the grey steel shafts of 86, 87 and 105.
- **Clearest cases:** 89, 90, 91, 93, 94, 98 and 100; also 134's hub, 143's wheel boss and 153's pivot disk.
- **Capture:** `tiles/94.png`, `tiles/89.png`, `tiles/93.png`, `z/m9091.png`, `z/m92-100-104.png`, `z/143b/143-front.png`.
- **Fix:** give shafts, pins and bolts a separate mid-grey steel material (≈ #8f9699, as in 86 and 87), keeping `ink` for true ink-dark details. This is a palette change, so it needs a sweep of the whole project, not just this range.

### 109 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the work screw has a finished thread along its whole length in every phase. Brown draws the thread cut only above the cutter, with the plain blank below; that is the point of the plate.
- **Flaw (low):** the work thread's pitch is 1.33× the lead screw's (24:32 change gears). Brown draws them about equal.
- **Capture:** `z/plate109.png`, `z/109/m109s.png`, `tiles/109.png`.
- **Fix:** drive the existing cut/blank boundary (`cutTurn` in `mujoco-thread-cutting/workpiece.js`) from the carriage position, so the groove exists only on the traversed side. Reset it on the return stroke, or retract the tool. For the pitch, use equal gears or document the ratio.

### 115 (medium; documented as impossible, but fixable)
- **Flaw:** both 12-tooth pinions and both racks still read as pointed stars. Brown draws square, flat-topped teeth.
- **Why it is fixable:** 114 and 118 already manage square teeth.
- **Capture:** `z/m115.png`, `tiles/115.png`.
- **Fix:** use a 12–14.5° pressure angle with a stub addendum (about 0.7 m) and a larger module, dropping to 10–11 teeth if needed. Give the racks 114's square profile.

### 116 (medium)
- **Flaw:** both pawls are thin grey strips about as wide as their pivot pin, which is the flimsy-pawl pattern. Brown draws a broad crescent plate with an eye.
- **Flaw (low):** each frame stem is two mismatched pieces (`leftStub` + `stubExtension0`, `rightStub` + `stubExtension1`), with visible steps at the ring. The extensions and `shaftTail0` cast no shadow.
- **Capture:** `z/116/m116p.png`, `z/116/m116r.png`, `z/116/m116s.png`, `z/plate116r.png`.
- **Fix:**
  - Make each pawl one smooth two-arc crescent extrusion with an eyed boss, its tip cut to the valley angle and seated in the root. Use one shared shape for both pawls.
  - Make each stem one extrusion or lathe flaring into the ring, with `castShadow` on.

### 133 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the platen crossbar floats in front of the frame columns in depth. There is a clear air gap in side view, so nothing visibly guides it. The top head also has a separate forward shelf that overhangs.
- **Capture:** `z/133/m133.png` (panel 3 for the gap; panels 1–2 for the shelf), `z/133/m133s.png`.
- **Fix:** give the platen end ears or straps that wrap the columns, or deepen the columns forward so the platen ends run in them. Trim the shelf.

### 143 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the worm's hand is reversed. The plate's front threads slope "/" (left-hand); the model's front crests slope "\" (`wormHandedness = 1` at `src/simulation/authored-screws.js:7718`). I verified this in a straight-on zoom.
- **Capture:** `z/143/cmp-lead.png` (plate beside the model), `z/143b/crop.png`, `z/143/m143.png`.
- **Fix:** set `wormHandedness = -1`, then re-derive the wheel's rotation sign and the traverse reversal, and recheck the mesh phase.

### 145 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the beam's left end is cut square, and the rod pin's brass ring overhangs the beam's top and end edges (edge-mount ratio 0.89). Brown draws a rounded eye concentric with the pin.
- **Flaw:** the upright rod's square top pokes above the beam: a box corner.
- **Capture:** `z/145/145-zp.png`, `z/145/145-zp2.png`.
- **Fix:** end the beam in a semicircular eye concentric with the pin, of radius ≥ 1.3× the ring. Round the rod's top concentric with the pin, or stop it inside the eye.

### 151 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the worm wheel has 18 coarse, blobby teeth. Brown draws about 36 fine teeth (hatch spacing ≈ 28 px on a radius of ≈ 160 px). The teeth also carry a 71° smoothed crease.
- **Flaw (low):** the hub collars are thin discs standing apart from the wheel. The plain middle of the screw shaft is about half the thread diameter, where Brown draws it about as thick.
- **Capture:** `z/151-cmp.png`, `tiles/151.png`.
- **Fix:**
  - Use about 36 teeth at a finer module, scaling the worm lead with it, and flat-shade the flanks.
  - Make the hub one boss touching both wheel faces.
  - Thicken the plain shaft section.

### 153 (medium; thin arms open since p90)
- **Flaw:** the elbow lever's lower arm jogs forward out of plane in a Z bend to reach the disk studs, an undrawn 3D protrusion.
- **Flaw:** both arms are about half Brown's width.
- **Flaw:** the elbow eye is hidden under an oversized black pivot disk.
- **Capture:** `z/153-lever.png`, `views/153-a-def.png`.
- **Fix:** make the lever one flat 2D extrusion in the stud plane, with plate-width arms, arc ends and a normal eye and pin. Put the bar's front stud in the same plane, and recheck the contacts in the bake.

### 155 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the pawl's upper prong ends in a flat square cut exactly at Brown's crop line. The caption describes a reversible pawl ("either direction according to the side on which the pawl works"), so a second nose is implied.
- **Flaw (low):** the rod ends at the top in an undrawn T-block with an eye, poking above the box.
- **Capture:** `z/155-pawl.png`, `z/155-rodtop.png`.
- **Fix:**
  - Complete a symmetric double-nosed pawl as one smooth extrusion, mirrored about the pivot's radial line. The working nose already seats in the root.
  - End the rod plainly.

### 168 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the slotted crank is a thin hairpin whose walls are thinner than the pin. Brown draws a broad arm with a narrow slot and a large shaft boss.
- **Capture:** `z/168-crank.png`.
- **Fix:** rebuild the crank as one extrusion: arm width about 2.5× the pin, a slot just wider than the pin, and a large boss. Keep the slot running through the shaft side if the elliptical pin path needs it, and say so.
