## Findings per movement

### 269 — high
- At each end of every tooth group, the rack teeth are depth-cut into several pieces where the pinion sweeps through them: a thin 0.046 back wafer ("full-outline web behind pinion"), a low striped "web backing strip" and a half-depth "transition tooth". In rotated views these read as notched, stepped teeth with flat hatched slabs; Brown draws plain full teeth. This is the hidden depth trick the rules forbid, since it hides that the pinion's teeth would cross those teeth in the plane, and the ledger does not mention it. Captures: `/dev/shm/p90/d/d1/269/wt.png`, `/dev/shm/p90/d/d1/269/rt.png`.
  - **Fix:** make every rack tooth one full-depth extrusion, and clear the pinion in the plane instead (tip-relieve or shorten the end teeth in the 2D profile, or re-phase the tooth groups). Delete the `*-relieved-tooth-full-outline-web-behind-pinion`, `*-web-backing-strip` and `*-relieved-transition-tooth` meshes in `src/simulation/authored-mutilated-racks.js`.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Shadow aliasing (renderer-wide):** shadow-map aliasing makes jagged or hairy shadow edges on curved parts at zoom (268, 275, 281, 305), and there is speckle that looks like shadow acne at close zoom (264). A global shadow bias/resolution review would clear it everywhere, instead of fixing each movement separately.
- **Hidden depth tricks in racks:** 269's depth-split rack teeth are the depth-offset trick the rules forbid. Other mutilated-rack and mangle movements outside this range should be checked for the same `relieved`/`web-behind-pinion` meshes.
- **Undrawn arbors:** empty bores (296, 320) and floating fixed pivot studs (308, 309) are one recurring class in this range, both in the escapement family and in the pulley pairs.
