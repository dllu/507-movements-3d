# Rolling mechanisms 365 and 373

Sources: [365](https://507movements.com/mm_365.html) and
[373](https://507movements.com/mm_373.html), engraving and caption. Neither
page provides a canvas animation. Intended cylindrical surfaces are analytical;
tracing their hand-drawn boundaries would introduce unwanted irregularity.

365 retains the compatible opposite roller spins and the resulting axial and
rotational stock velocities. The crossed cylinders meet at points, not lines.
Raised rod dots and roller stripes have become surface paint, so motion cues no
longer protrude into the rolling interface. Marks fade at the stock-window ends
before recycling. The unsupported invented stand and guide collars are hidden;
the source-facing view exposes the roller crossing. A roller turn takes at
least ten seconds in playback.

373 now has four connected drum spokes, solid carriage disks with actual axle
bores, an actual belt channel, and a chassis behind the wheels. The loads leave
space for the removable teaching weight, which lands on the bed. Fixed axles
and the front support frame connect the apparatus. The indicator has a bored
face and a housing input passage, rather than a tether penetrating its face.
Its previously exposed spring is hidden because a radial tether does not
explain the assumed torque arm. The retained spring/force state is explicitly a
calibration, not a solved internal transmission. The existing eight-second
load/speed demonstration and exact rolling-speed ratio are preserved.

Validation: 20 focused checks cover the existing analytical laws plus 25-pose
selected finite-surface checks, actual rolling-contact proximity, bores, wheel
attachments, and surface-marker wrap fading. Source/default and oblique browser
captures show no errors; the full-cycle projected bounds remain within the
viewport (maximum NDC .708 for 365 and .895 for 373).

Limits: 365 still prescribes synchronization and assumes no slip; preload,
friction, compliance and drive details are unspecified. 373 illustrates the
reported speed-independent resistance with an empirical load law. It does not
solve carriage reactions, inertia, slip, or the indicator's internal transmission.
The weight is manually moved as a teaching aid. Selected geometric checks are
not a full dynamics validation. No new live physics or per-frame mesh allocation
is introduced.
