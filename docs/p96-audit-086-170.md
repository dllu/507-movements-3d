# Pass 96 audit, lane p96-b: movements 86–170

Reviewer: Claude Opus 5.5, lane p96-b (merged from four forked sub-lanes: 86–106, 107–127, 128–148 and 149–170). Date: 2026-09-28. Read-only audit of HEAD `7ce1e83`.

All captures and screens are in `/dev/shm/p96/b/`, outside Git. Paths below are relative to that directory.

- **Tiles:** `tiles/<ID>.png` holds, in a 3×3 grid, the plate, the default view, +50° yaw/+20° pitch, −50°/−15°, behind (180°/+10°), left (90°), right (−90°), top (+75° pitch) and below (−60° pitch).
- **Cycle strips:** `tiles/<ID>-cycle.png` holds 12 phases (0, 1/12, …, 11/12) at the default view, for checking motion over the whole cycle.
- **Raw views:** `views/`.
- **Zooms:** `z/<ID>/` and `z/`, made with `shots.mjs` and its `VIEWS`, `ZOOM`, `AT` and `HIDE` environment variables.
- **Screens:** `disc.json`, `cf.json`, `white.json`, `edge.json` and `facet.json`, from the five `scripts/screen-*.mjs` screens run over 86–170.
- **Probes:** `probe.mjs` (mesh bounding boxes), `ray91.mjs` (91's cam–yoke clearance) and `worm.mjs` (143's thread hand).
- **Ledger rows:** `ledger.md`.

Every movement in 86–170 was viewed in all eight views and its 12-phase strip, beside the plate. Suspect spots were zoomed. Each ledger row, and the p93, p94 and p95 docs, were read so that fixed or documented forced flaws are not re-reported. The "ink shafts read as holes" palette question is excluded, as the brief says.

## Screen results

- **Coincident faces:**
  - The only pair with a visible area is 136: the tooth-ring valley floor is coplanar with the cam wheel's base (area 0.089, contrast 0.04). It flickers faintly (see 136).
  - The other pairs are too small or too low-contrast to see: 87 (2.5e-5), 115, 120–123, 125 and 126 (< 1e-6), and 133 (contrast 0.04, hidden).
- **Disconnected parts:**
  - No floating parts.
  - 132's bed block and 127's pump barrels stand on their own feet, as documented.
  - 88's "detached" group is the bare cam, hub and shaft, which is correct.
  - Lips: 92 is 0.0085 on the undrawn `rearSupport` (see 92). 152 is 0.094 on the groove wall and 161 is 0.097 on the lower support (both reported). 140's 0.015 lip is hidden in the guide mouth.
  - The short-of-pin and near-miss rows are running or bore clearances; the zooms show them seated.
  - The screen output has no entries for 151, 167, 169 and 170. Their captures show nothing detached.
- **White parts:** only 152's parchment board (#e9e1cf), which is intended.
- **Edge mounts:**
  - 87's `reversingStud` (ratio 0.65) is reported. 87's `bellRodPin` (0.86) is not visible at normal zoom.
  - 133's knob sits at the crank end, as drawn.
  - 145's beam-end ring no longer flags. Its opposite, pivot end is reported by eye.
- **Faceting:** 151's upper worm shaft (20.8°) is visible and reported. Every other flag was zoomed and is not visible: 86, 89, 98, 100, 104, 105, 110, 114, 116, 153, 155, and 159's disk and treadle.
- **91, checked numerically:** a ray test over 200 phases shows the Reuleaux cam never penetrates the yoke; the minimum clearance is 0.041. The overlap seen at ±50° is the yoke's bevel.
- **143, checked numerically:** on the production mesh the worm's crest rises "/" on the front face, which is left-handed and matches the plate. The p93 handedness fix holds.
- **151, checked:** with the other parts hidden, the thread hands are "" on the left and "/" on the right, matching the plate.

## Findings (ordered by ID)

### 87 (low; open since p93)
- **Flaw:** the yellow reversing stud on wheel E still sits on the inner edge of the band, and its boss overhangs into the spoke window (edge ratio 0.65). Brown puts it mid-band.
- **Capture:** `z/87/m3.png` (e1, top middle), `z/m104-87.png` (panels 3–4).
- **Fix:** move the stud radially to the band's mid-radius, or shrink the boss to about 0.8× the band width. Then re-time its strike on G's arm, which ends at the same radius.

### 90, 91 (low; open since p90)
- **Flaw:** the yoke stems are still round rods. Brown draws flat bars filleted into the yoke: the stubs at 90's left and right, and 91's top and bottom.
- **Capture:** `tiles/90.png`, `tiles/91.png`.
- **Fix:** give the stems a rectangular section in the same extrusion as the yoke, with fillets at the junction. The round guides can stay as they are.

### 92 (medium; open since p90)
- **Flaw:** the undrawn grey `rearSupport` bar (x −0.2…0.98, z −0.38…−0.15) still runs from the guide's end to the wheel's shaft. It is visible in the DEFAULT view: through the gap between the wheel and the guide, at hub height, in phases 0.08–0.25 and 0.58–0.92. It is also visible from behind, and it carries the 0.0085 lip.
- **Capture:** `tiles/92-cycle.png` (p01–p03, p07–p11), `tiles/92.png` (d-back).
- **Fix:** delete it, as the ledger already says undrawn guide hardware is not shown. If a support must stay, drop it into a thin plate kept entirely inside the wheel's silhouette, behind the rear hub (z < −0.14, |y| < 0.25), so it can't be seen past the rim.

### 99 (low; open since p93)
- **Flaw:** the two frame rails are 0.09-square wire posts, 2.4 long, that end free at both ends. They read flimsy beside the 0.9-wide guide block, which reaches them only through brackets behind.
- **Capture:** `tiles/99.png` (b-rotp50, h-below).
- **Fix:** thicken the rails to about 0.16 so they read as Brown's double-line bars. Optionally close the brackets into the block's own extrusion.

### 102, 103 (low; open since p90)
- **Flaw:** the square threads are still deep, thin fins with lands of about ⅓ of the pitch. In 102 the thread depth is about 0.18 of the outer diameter, against about 0.10 on the plate. The hand ("\", right-hand) matches the plate.
- **Capture:** `z/102/m.png` (plate beside a model zoom), `z/102/platecrop.png`, `tiles/103.png`.
- **Fix:** use lands of about 0.5 × pitch and a depth of about 0.5 × pitch (square-thread proportions), keeping the nut and T-nut bores matched.

### 107 (low)
- **Flaw:** the serpentine groove is coarser than the plate's. The model shows about 5 turn-arounds across the visible face (a six-stroke groove); the plate shows about 8. The turn-arounds also read slightly pointed rather than evenly rounded.
- **Capture:** `z/107/cmp.png`, `z/107/m.png`.
- **Fix:** raise the stroke count to 8–10 per revolution, if the follower's contact allows it, and use a pure sinusoid along the unrolled circumference. Otherwise document the count as forced.

### 109 (low)
- **Flaw 1:** at the top of the stroke (phase ≈0.75) the carriage's top face sits flush against the underside of the top frame bar, so it reads as colliding.
- **Flaw 2:** the lead-screw thread ends at the top in a thin sharp fin (the same run-out pattern as 111).
- **Capture:** `z/109/m.png`, `tiles/109-cycle.png` (p09).
- **Fix:** trim the stroke, or lower the top bar by about one carriage-clearance so a visible gap remains. Square off the thread run-out.

### 110 (low)
- **Flaw:** the two nut arms differ in shape. The over-nut arm is a broad flat bar lying on the nut's top. The under-nut arm is a thin tapered strip running to the underside of the open half-nut. Brown draws two identical I-section arms. The caption does require one half-nut over the roller and one under, so only the arm shape is at fault.
- **Capture:** `z/110/m2.png` (panel 2 shows both arms side by side), `z/110/m.png`.
- **Fix:** use one arm extrusion of the same section for both, mirrored top and bottom, running straight from the sleeve to the centre of its nut.

### 111 (low)
- **Flaw 1:** the square-thread run-outs of both screws end in thin tapering blades that poke out beside the ends (the bottom-left of the orange sleeve; the top in p03 and p09).
- **Flaw 2:** a fuzzy, aliased line runs along the root of the orange thread, where it meets the core.
- **Capture:** `z/111/m.png` (panel 1), `tiles/111-cycle.png`.
- **Fix:**
  - End each thread with a square cut, or a short radial ramp, flush with the end face, in the shared `helicalThread` helper.
  - Sink the thread's inner face slightly into the core (by about 1e-3 of the radius) so the root seam doesn't fight.

### 113 (low)
- **Flaw 1:** the two table rollers are plain grey discs with no rotation cue. The rule is that featureless rotating parts need the quadrant cue.
- **Flaw 2:** the rack teeth are slightly pointed trapezoids, where Brown draws them square.
- **Capture:** `z/113/m.png`, `tiles/113.png`.
- **Fix:** apply the shared odd/even quadrant face colouring to both rollers, and use the square rack profile from 114 or 118.

### 116 (low)
- **Flaw 1:** the pawls, although now crescents with an eye, are still narrow slivers: their greatest width is about the diameter of the eye. The rear pawl is barely visible from behind. Brown's pawl is a broad crescent.
- **Flaw 2:** the left stem is about twice the diameter of the right one. Brown draws them nearly equal.
- **Capture:** `z/116/m.png` (z1, z4), `z/116/m2.png`.
- **Fix:** widen the crescent's outer arc so its mid-width is about 1.5× the eye, keeping the seated tip. Bring the two stem diameters within about 20% of each other.

### 117 (medium)
- **Flaw:** each friction roller is a long cylinder standing wholly in front of the yoke. It is carried at one end only, by a thin wire-like arc bracket, so it reads as a cantilevered drum on a hoop. The far end has nothing under it in the rotated and back views.
- **Capture:** `z/117/m.png` (panels 2–3), `tiles/117.png` (b-rotp50, e-left).
- **Fix:** give each roller a proper fork of two cheek plates, one on each face of the roller, joined to the crossbar (a pulley-block yoke). Alternatively, shorten each roller to the cam's thickness and bring it into the yoke's plane. Either way, make the fork plate at least half the roller's diameter wide.

### 118 (low)
- **Flaw:** the fixed rack base has a rounded right end but a square left end. Brown draws matching curved ends.
- **Capture:** `tiles/118.png`.
- **Fix:** mirror the right end's round onto the left end.

### 122 and 125 (low)
- **Flaw:** each connecting rod has the same colour as the gear it drives: blue on blue, gold on gold, orange on orange. Where a rod crosses its gear's face (122 p08–p10; 125 p01, p08) the crank and rod merge and the linkage is hard to read.
- **Capture:** `tiles/122-cycle.png`, `tiles/125-cycle.png`.
- **Fix:** give the rods a contrasting link colour (for example the gold or grey used for the beams), or a darker shade of the gear colour.

### 128 (low, open since p93)
- **Flaw:** the frame's side lugs are pointed wedges with a flat tip. Brown draws rounded knobs on a short neck.
- **Capture:** `tiles/128.png`.
- **Fix:** give each lug a neck plus a circular-arc knob in the same frame extrusion.

### 129 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the hook hangs from a single strap on the sheave's front face only. From behind, and at +150° yaw, the hook shank comes out under the rim with nothing holding it, and the sheave axle has no rear cheek. The load is carried off-centre on one side. This was reported low in p93 and is still open.
- **Flaw (low):** the strap and hook are the same blue as the sheave, so the block does not read as a separate part.
- **Capture:** `z/129-z.png` (panel 1 is the rear view with the unsupported shank; panel 2 is the one-sided front strap), `tiles/129.png`.
- **Fix:** make a symmetric two-cheek clevis (front and rear straps joined below the rim into one block that carries the hook eye), with the axle through both cheeks. Give it a contrasting colour, for example the steel grey used for the frame.

### 131 (medium)
- **Flaw:** the tooth forms do not match each other or the plate. The sector teeth are rounded finger lobes, while the rack teeth are sharp pointed triangles. Brown draws both with the same square, flat-topped teeth. Open since p93 (low).
- **Flaw (low):** at phase ≈0.42 (p05) the rack's left end is only just flush with the outside of the left guide block. Brown draws the bar running well past both guides.
- **Capture:** `z/131-z.png` (panel 3 compares the teeth; panels 1–2 show the rack end at p05), `tiles/131-cycle.png`.
- **Fix:** use one conjugate stub or square profile, with the ~11 sector teeth on the plate, and rescale the rack pitch to match. Lengthen the rack about 0.3 at its left end.

### 132 (low; documented, but fixable)
- **Flaw:** from +50° the platen block floats clear of the columns, with nothing guiding it (the ledger says "hangs from the lower disc with no guide").
- **Capture:** `tiles/132.png` (b-rotp50).
- **Fix:** reuse 133's p93 treatment: add end ears or straps that wrap the columns.

### 134 (medium, open since p90)
- **Flaw:** Brown's segmented lagging rim (hatched segments between radial blocks) and the large hub ring are still missing. The model is a plain four-spoke grooved pulley, so the drawn parts are absent.
- **Capture:** `tiles/134.png` (the plate beside the default view).
- **Fix:** add the lagging as one extrusion (an outer ring with 8 radial block notches, as drawn) and a hub ring of about 0.25 R. Keep the rope groove in the lagging.

### 136 (low)
- **Flaw:** the coincident-face screen flags the tooth ring's valley floor lying coplanar with the cam wheel's base face (area 0.089, 48 triangle pairs, all four phases). The contrast is only 0.04, so it flickers faintly in the tooth valleys when orbiting.
- **Capture:** `cf.json` (id 136), `tiles/136.png`.
- **Fix:** merge the tooth ring and the base disc into one solid, or sink the teeth 0.002 into the base.

### 144 (medium, open since p93)
- **Flaw:** the post stands behind the lazy tongs. Brown dots the tongs' links where they pass behind the post, so the post belongs in front, on its own plane.
- **Capture:** `tiles/144.png` (the plate's dotted links inside the post beside the default view), `z/144-z.png`.
- **Fix:** move the post in front of the tongs, with the fixed centre pin passing back into it, and make the post see-through in the shared style so the pinned joint still reads.

### 145 (low)
- **Flaw:** the beam's right (pivot) end is still cut square, with the brass pivot ring almost touching the end face. Brown draws a rounded end concentric with the hatched pivot. The left end was rounded in p93.
- **Capture:** `tiles/145.png` (the default view beside the plate).
- **Fix:** end the beam in a semicircular eye concentric with the pivot, like the left end.

### 147 (low)
- **Flaw:** each fan is a plain plate that the round arm simply runs across to mid-face, with no fastening. Brown draws the arm ending in a flat strap bolted (two holes) to the fan frame.
- **Capture:** `z/147-z.png`.
- **Fix:** end each arm in a flat strap lying on the fan face, with two bolt heads, in one extrusion with the arm.

### 148 (low)
- **Flaw:** the lever's short tail ends in an open ring, an empty eye with the background showing through, that attaches to nothing. The working groove pin is at the elbow.
- **Capture:** `z/148-z.png`.
- **Fix:** close the tail end as a solid rounded boss, or trim the tail back to the elbow if the plate's end circle is the same pin.

### 150 (low)
- **Flaw:** the pinned lever is not flat in its drawn plane. It runs diagonally in depth from the roller, which sits at the stack's rear cam, to the pivot eye at the front. The skew is obvious from the top and in rotated views; Brown draws one flat lever.
- **Capture:** `z/150/lv.png` (left panel: top view), `z/150/side.png`.
- **Fix:** put the lever in one plane at the roller's depth, and lengthen the fixed pivot stud (or the roller's stud) to span the depth offset. The roller then rides the selected cam with the lever flat.

### 151 (low)
- **Flaw:** the black upper worm shaft reads as faceted: polygonal flats and highlight bands show in rotated zooms (the faceting screen gives 20.8° edges on `horizontal-input-worm`).
- **Flaw:** a thin brass arc (a washer rim) peeks out around the top of the shaft end inside the grey collar.
- **Flaw:** the nuts carry undrawn side "guide tongue" blocks (`nut-guide-tongue-0/1`), visible in the left, right and ±50° views. Brown draws plain rectangular nuts.
- **Capture:** `z/151/151-worm.png` (right panel of `z/159/rim.png`), `z/151/151-zl.png`, `tiles/151.png` (e-left, f-right).
- **Fix:**
  - Raise the radial segment count on the worm shaft and core (≥ 48), or use smooth normals on the cylinder sides.
  - Shrink the washer to sit flush inside the collar, or drop it.
  - Hide the guide tongues; the nuts are already constrained by the kinematics.

### 152 (low)
- **Flaw:** seen from below at an oblique angle, a light hairline runs the length of the horizontal groove's dark floor. It is a seam or gap between the groove floor and the wall pieces. The screen also measures a 0.094 lip of the horizontal groove wall over the cross base.
- **Capture:** `z/152/arm.png` (right panel).
- **Fix:** make the grooved cross one extrusion (outline minus the two slots) with a single floor, so there are no separate floor or wall pieces to open a seam.

### 154 (low)
- **Flaw:** the weight barely moves: its ball bobs only about 1% of the view height over the cycle, and the bell crank's upper arm swings a few degrees. The caption's "weighted cord" return reads as static.
- **Capture:** `tiles/154-cycle.png`.
- **Fix:** lengthen the studs' reach or shorten the crank's lower arm, so each stud swings the crank about 20–30° and the weight visibly rises and drops. At minimum, record the stroke in the ledger.

### 161 (low; still open from p93)
- **Flaw:** the lower support is still the open-slotted fork, and it still overhangs the lower bearing by 0.097 (screen lip).
- **Capture:** `tiles/161.png` (default and ±50°).
- **Fix:** make it one solid tapered extrusion flush with the bearing boss.

### 164 (low; still open since p90)
- **Flaw:** nothing but the lever and the knee link moves. Across the 12-phase strip the top plank and the foot block stay put within a pixel or two, so the press produces no visible output.
- **Capture:** `tiles/164-cycle.png`.
- **Fix:** move the platen (the top plank, or the block) along the knee's line of action with the toggle, amplified if needed. Otherwise document the stroke explicitly as a limit.

### 166 (low; also 153; still open from p93)
- **Flaw:** the quadrant speed-cue boundaries on the disk still render as thin dark hairlines, which read like drawn notation at 3×.
- **Capture:** `z/166-hub.png`.
- **Fix:** build the cue as vertex colours on one mesh, with no shared-edge seam.

### 168, 169 (low)
- **Flaw:** each crank and rocker shaft projects about two crank-lengths behind the linkage as a long grey shaft with two large bearing collars. It dominates the left, right, top and below views; Brown draws only the shaft ends.
- **Capture:** `tiles/168.png` (e-left, g-top), `tiles/169.png`, `z/168/slot.png`.
- **Fix:** shorten the shafts to just past one slim bearing, or hide them behind the cranks, keeping only the end-on boss.
