## 185 Locomotive Stephenson link — fixed

**Brown's measurements.** The link is 85 px overall against a 66 px pin spacing, which is 1.29×. The
lifting lug sits 22.5 px (0.47) left of the slot.

**Why it failed before.** With opposed eccentrics, the link rocks ±0.49 rad about that offset lug. This
makes the die slip ±0.056–0.064 rad along the slot, whatever the gear. At the old 0.45 rad reversing
arc, full gear put the die centre at 0.17 rad, the pin angle, so it over-ran the pins to 0.236.

**Change** (`authored-locomotive-valve-gears.js`):
- The slot half-angle is 0.1874, giving Brown's 1.29× overall.
- The die is a square block, 0.20 on a side.
- The largest die angle that stays clear of the closed ends is 0.1468.
- The reversing arc is 0.22 rad. This matches Brown's shorter notched quadrant: 122 px, against 205 px
  before and his roughly 125 px.
- `sourceSelector` is −0.368. It keeps Brown's source lever angle (−0.081 rad), so the source pose is
  unchanged.

**Die range per gear:**

| Gear | Die range (rad) |
| --- | --- |
| Full forward | [0.027, 0.144] |
| Full backward | [−0.121, −0.029] |
| Mid gear | [−0.050, 0.056] |

The whole block stays within ±0.168, inside the pins at 0.1695.

**Cost.** The full-gear valve strokes fall from 0.46–0.50 to 0.24–0.27, against 0.126 in mid gear. The
forward and backward valve signals correlate at −0.66 (it was −0.94). The gear still reverses the valve
and the engine. The tests pin these new values, with comments.

**Evidence.** `ba185.png`, and `v185/` (link zoom at t = 0, 0.2–0.25 full forward and 0.68–0.73 full
backward).

**Intersections** (129 samples, before and after run back to back):
- Removed: `upper-closed-slot-end × die-body` 0.0023, where the die hit the slot end, and
  `upper-suspension-pin × valve link` 0.0100.
- All other pairs are the same shared-pin layering overlaps (≤0.094, unchanged).

**Seam.** Clean.

## 159 Cord-and-pulley treadle — improved

Slack itself is forced: a taut cord would put the foot 133 px below the floor. Brown's crank needs
146 px of cord travel, and the treadle can give less from the floor to Brown's pose.

What changed is where the slack goes (`mujoco-cord-treadle/ideal-cord-shape.js`):
- Before, it went into an S-bend in the vertical run.
- Now the vertical run hangs straight from the pulley to the resting treadle's eye. Its weight draws the
  cord over the free pulley.
- The slack settles in the diagonal run as a gravity sag: a parabola with a vertical axis from the pin.
  It meets the pulley tangentially, and the entry point is solved.
- The pulley angle follows the cord.

**Rebake and reports.**
- The baked `amplitude` channel (now the sag) was rebaked: `scripts/bake-cord-treadle.mjs`, closure
  4e-13.
- `docs/validation/159-ideal-shape*.json` were regenerated.
- The self-clearance review script now takes the quadratic curvature. Rope radius × curvature is at most
  0.67, self clearance 0.087 and floor clearance 0.96.

**Evidence.** `v159b/` (8 phases): the slack shows as a hanging sag or U under the crank pin for about
30% of the turn, with the vertical run straight.

**Intersections.** Production screen, 33 samples, before and after run back to back. The same pairs
remain: the cord ending in the pin (0.073) and in the treadle eye (0.070–0.071). These are the
attachments, not new contacts.

**Tests.** All cord-treadle tests pass. The ideal-shape test was rewritten for the sag.

**Residual.** The slack is still visible as a deep sag for part of the turn.

## 455 Old rotary pump — reclassified; no geometry change

A 2D widest-path flow check was scripted at 0.0005 resolution (`/dev/shm/p72-b/leak455.mjs`). It
rasterizes the front sections of the casing, drum and valves at a given time. Then it finds the largest
gap on any path from the inlet side (0, −1.45) to the exit side (2.0, −0.3).

Results:
- Over a half cycle every 0.05 s, the widest path never exceeds 0.0060. That is the corner's running
  clearance.
- Across the groove's crossing of the corner (t 1.36–1.50, every 0.02 s) it is 0.0057, the same value.
- With a valve bearing, it is 0.0020.

The groove connects to the recess behind the valve only through the knuckle's 0.006 socket clearance.
That path exists at the hinge line for any hinged valve with running clearance. So the groove opens no
leak wider than the running clearance.

The groove is 3.5° wide on the drum surface. At the default scale it is not visible: see
`c455.png`, a 4× crop of the hinge.

## 137 Expansion eccentric — forced

Brown's traced cam edge (`src/data/expansion-eccentric-outline.js`) was measured through the shaft. Its
diametral breadth r(θ) + r(θ + π) varies from 144 px (θ = 150°/330°) to 170 px (75°/255°). That is
±13 px.

The two fork rollers sit 212 px apart on a line through the shaft, and the fork is a rigid arm. Any cam
bearing on both needs constant breadth to within the running gap, about 1 px. Brown's dimples sit
nearly opposite other dimples (150° against 330°), not opposite bumps, so they cannot be kept.

The current cam fits the landmarks to 3.93 px RMS while holding the breadth. Neither smaller rollers nor
play changes the breadth condition. No change.
