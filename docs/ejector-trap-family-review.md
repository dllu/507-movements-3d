# Ejectors and steam traps: 475–478

Primary references: [475 Brear bilge ejector](https://507movements.com/mm_475.html), [476 Lansdell steam siphon](https://507movements.com/mm_476.html), [477 Hoard & Wiggin diaphragm trap](https://507movements.com/mm_477.html), and [478 Ray expansion trap](https://507movements.com/mm_478.html). All four official pages mark animation unavailable and contain no animation model. The captions and engravings therefore supply the operating sequence and topology; physical dimensions, operating pressures, thermal timing and display motion remain reconstruction assumptions.

## Corrections

A shared `ejector-trap-working-parts.js` reuses finite pipe walls, turned sections, bored plates and closed screw threads. It retains all existing thermal, volume, flow and quasi-static contact laws. Each model now retains its authored 4, 4, 6 or 8 second cycle through display timing, disables ground/fog and uses a source-facing view fitted through the complete cycle.

- **475:** Suction and discharge pipes have real bores instead of capped cylinders. The mixing chamber has finite thickness and a side opening for the steam feed. The nozzle approaches vertically; its bore clears the steam core. Actual steam/water meshes clear the selected chamber, pipe and nozzle surfaces.
- **476:** A unioned finite fork cutaway replaces overlapping tubes that obstructed the water junction. Both lower mouths and the upper outlet are open; the lower mouths extend into the displayed supply. The steam feed passes behind the right water branch and enters through an opening in the rear section, matching the engraving. Its terminal segment turns vertically into the discharge and clears the water and steam paths.
- **477:** The sealed moving valve has one finite hollow body around its working-liquid display, a finite diaphragm, and an actual conical seat matching the existing maximum lift. The former toroidal seat missed the shoulder. The bridge reacts against a rounded cap while its feet meet the box floor; the rear wall clears the moving body. The former static flow tubes crossed the new seat, so they are hidden and finite moving markers follow the changing annular gap, shrinking linearly with flow to fit the closing passage. There is no fluid-geometry rebuild during playback.
- **478:** The sphere has finite wall thickness and open pipe/plunger/outlet ports. The expanding pipe and stuffing box have real bores. A flat pipe end meets the finite valve face at the existing analytical closing state. The lever roller contacts the side of its pad instead of occupying its center. The lever has a bored pivot and a supported stand; the fixed screw-stop post reaches the base. The adjusting screw has a closed thread and a rounded stop tip, preserving the prescribed lever and stop law.

## Validation

`tests/ejector-trap-working-solids.test.mjs` adds ten tests. Selected actual mesh surfaces are sampled in both directions, with 65 cycle poses for the moving traps: valve/seat, diaphragm/bridge, moving assembly/case, stuffing/rod, pipe/valve face, lever/pivot, roller/pad, screw/stop, and displayed flow/passage interfaces. The static ejectors are checked against their actual finite water/steam envelopes. Additional assertions verify outward-wound material around real bores, seated/open conical geometry, pipe/valve contact, the stop's base support, stable mesh identities, and readable timing. Existing 38 analytical/continuity tests remain; three expectations were updated for an endpoint floating-point tolerance, corrected rearward steam approach, and the marker's new linear closing scale.

The combined suite passed 48/48. The final support-only correction is also covered by the focused rerun. Initial browser source/default/front/advanced views and 17-pose full-geometry framing checks showed no errors or clipping; final views are saved in `/dev/shm/ejector22-final-*`. No generated review images are committed. A local 250-update timing sample of the new 477 marker routing measured 0.45 ms median / 0.78 ms P95; rendering cost is separate.

## Limits

Geometry does not solve fluid dynamics. Ejector chamber pressure is supplied by the existing steady reconstruction; vacuum startup, condensation, turbulence, diffuser recovery, cavitation and losses are not simulated. Trap temperature histories and quasi-static expansion/contact are prescribed; heat transfer, valve inertia, friction, leakage, condensate inventory and pressure transients remain unintegrated. The finite fork and chamber sections are deliberately visible cutaways, not a watertight/manufacturing qualification. Selected neighbor checks do not establish exhaustive all-pairs collision freedom. No native contact solve was warranted for the retained determinate thermal displacement and geometric closure in this bounded pass.
