# Chain beam and upright engine pass: 342–343

Both [342](https://507movements.com/mm_342.html) and
[343](https://507movements.com/mm_343.html) have working official canvas models.
The engraving, caption and canvas construction were checked together. Their
existing analytic motion is retained: 342 corrects rounded canvas endpoints to
keep the continuous chain path constant; 343 simultaneously closes its three
rigid links instead of using the canvas's sequential approximate construction.
The existing dense closure and derivative tests remain applicable.

342 now has alternating bored chain plates, common pins and bored rollers in
the circular shoe's working plane. The terminal segment was too short for two
finite eyes; one fewer regular link leaves room for the final two-pin connector.
The main beam bearing, piston eye and pump-rod eye have real shaft passages.
The shaft spans its bearing, the piston rod joins its head, and the cylinder is
aligned with the piston and deep enough for the bottom of the stroke.

343 reuses the shared bored constant-length rods and finite plate geometry.
The connecting rod, radius rods, crosspiece and piston eye occupy separate
planes joined by pins. The flywheel sits behind the frame, the bored bearings
connect to its pillars, and the piston and cylinder share a working plane.
Cylinder feet connect the forward section to the foundation.
Both scenes use sampled full-cycle framing without a ground plane or fog.

## Evidence and limits

The four added finite-assembly tests sample 65 poses. They check piston/head
connection and cylinder containment, guide clearance, actual open pin passages,
shaft engagement, alternating chain-plate clearance, roller/shoe proximity and
343's moving/fixed depth layers. Existing tests check analytic constraints,
source differences, velocity and cycle closure. Desktop source comparisons and
oblique views cover both mechanisms; 17 sampled camera poses stay inside the
viewport (maximum absolute projected coordinate below 0.914).

342 still models an inextensible continuous chain path, not a dynamically
solved roller chain: straight plate chords vary slightly as their sampled
nodes pass around the shoe. The rollers make the intended finite contact
visible; they do not establish a rigid-link contact simulation. Pressure colors
are explanatory, not a thermodynamic calculation. Pump-side geometry beyond
the cropped engraving is inferred. Decorative shoe markings remain simplified.

343's output locus is intentionally nearly straight, not mathematically exact;
the piston guide allows its small lateral displacement. Open rectangular
cylinder sections, support webs and a completed uncropped flywheel are
explanatory reconstructions. These tests cover the identified working
interfaces, not every possible pair of solids or behavior under load. No new
live physics or manually traced contours are needed for this bounded pass.
